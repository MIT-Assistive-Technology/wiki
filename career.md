## Fall Career Fair
Check out the [website](https://capd.mit.edu/channels/fall-career-fair/).
- They release the layout (which companies at which booth and where) prior to the career fair.
  - I recommend having a "hit list" so you don't waste 3+ hours at career fair.
  - Many booths will have extremely long lines. Some booths, like Google, aren't really worth visiting.
- If you're a first year:
  - You get a 30 min head start over all other MIT students. Take advantage of this to collect the best merch (though not all companies will be set up yet, so beware) or to talk to popular companies.
  - Not all companies are "first-year-friendly." Check the list on the career fair website for companies labeled "FYF."
- Print out your resume and come prepared with a 30-second pitch about yourself.
- Bring a pen and extra piece of paper for taking notes (people's names, who you want to follow up with, etc).
- Wear formal clothes!

## Resume Checklist
General:
- I would recommend getting your resume looked at every year by CAPD, UPOP, etc.
  - You can sign up for resume review appointments with CAPD [here](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resumes/).
- I would also recommend keeping multiple versions of your resume, especially if you have a wide range of interests.
  - For example, an EECS major might want to have a more EE-oriented resume, CS oriented resume, robotics-oriented resume, etc.

Templates:
I recommmend these: [Jake's Resume](https://www.overleaf.com/latex/templates/jakes-resume/syzfjbzwjncs) or [Software Engineer Resume](https://www.overleaf.com/latex/templates/software-engineer-resume/gqxmqsvsbdjf)

Checklist: go through this **BEFORE** asking for a review from anyone.
- [ ] "Correct" grammar:
  - [ ] Capitalized words i.e., names
  - [ ] Periods at the end of each sentence
  - [ ] Correct spelling
  - [ ] Consistent verb tense
  - [ ] NOTE: sentence fragments are okay! Implied "I," because conciseness is key.
- [ ] Consistent formatting:
  - [ ] Consistent headings and bullet points
  - [ ] Consistent spacing and indentation
  - [ ] Position, Company -- Time, Location (for each experience)
- [ ] Good style:
  - [ ] Consistent tone (professional)
  - [ ] Concise wording
  - [ ] Easy to read
  - [ ] No unnecessary repetition
- [ ] Strong impact:
  - [ ] Main point is clear: what did YOU contribute
  - [ ] Most important information stands out
  - [ ] Claims are specific and supported (NUMBERS!!!)
